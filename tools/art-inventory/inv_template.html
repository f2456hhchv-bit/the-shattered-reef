Shattered Reef Art Inventory

Every piece of art in the game, checked against the new standard: the simple toy harbour (2026-10-08). Each asset shows how it draws in the game today, its style (simple toy, detailed toy, older painted, or code) and how urgently it needs redoing. Most of the first toy pass is too busy for the new standard, so this is a fresh pass at nearly everything. The prompt kit below is everything you need to brief ChatGPT, in one place.

Overview

The standard

A simple chunky toy, like a child's wooden or plastic playset piece. A few big rounded blocks (about 3-6 parts), thick silhouette, slightly squat cute proportions. Three or four flat matte colours, soft light from the top-left, smooth with no texture.

No clutter. No small props (barrels, crates, cannonball piles, ropes, chains, ladders, lanterns), no brick or plank lines, no rivets, scales, cracks, patterns, windows grids or text. If a part wouldn't be visible at 48px, leave it out.

The harbour mockup sets this standard; attach it to every request. The first toy pass (ships, buildings, towers, most creatures) is too detailed: same family, too many parts. Never use the word "painted" in a prompt.

Restyle priority, in order

  1. Terrain (batch I): 8 chart islands are in; Caverns and Crystal are left, and Tropical and Cliff & Cove want redoing top-down to match. No more land/shore tiles needed: the image tool makes shaped pads, not seamless textures, so the arena is restyled in code from their colours.
  2. Island props (batch P): palms, rocks and the rest, chunky enough to read at play size. Tropical first.
  3. Icons (batches D, E): 72 toy icons are in (74 emoji covered); 15 emoji still need art (batch D, 12 icons) and 5 are stand-ins (batch E). See Icons.
  4. Bosses (batch A): the busy ones only; Kraken's Anchor, Frost Leviathan and the Warding Seal already pass.
  5. Creatures (batch G): the busy ones only; sharks, jellies, squid, wisps, bats, serpent and troll already pass.
  6. Reef Defence towers (batch T) and landmarks (batch F).
  7. Low, when there's time: weather objects (W) and shots (X). Animated overlays, bars, rings and the UI are restyled in code, not drawn.
  8. Already simple, keep: harbour buildings (B), player ships (S, enemy ships follow), pickups (C) and effects (H).

Do

  • Attach the harbour mockup to every request (and, when redoing an asset, its current image so the shape carries over). The style follows the picture far more than the words.
  • Ask for one asset per message, on a transparent background (flat #FF00FF magenta if it can't).
  • TOP view for anything that swims or flies: straight down, front pointing to the top of the image. The game turns it to any heading.
  • FRONT view for upright things (golems, sirens, buildings, towers, landmarks, pickups, icons): front three-quarter, slightly from above. The game mirrors it left and right.
  • Ships only: one image with 5 views in a row (see the prompt kit).
  • ISLAND view (chart islands): one small round island seen from high above at a slight angle, its landmark on top, transparent sea around it.
  • Fill most of the frame; around 1024px is fine. The game trims and scales it.

Don't

  • Don't accept busy results: small props, plank or brick lines, ropes, chains, many windows. Reply with the fix line below rather than starting again.
  • Don't accept painterly or textured results either.
  • Don't send 24-frame turn sheets. They came back mostly stern-side and unusable; the 5-view set works.
  • Don't send 8-direction pre-rotations, or re-renders of the game's current art. Both changed nothing last time.
  • Don't bake in shadows, water, glows or text. The game draws its own.
  • Don't send terrain tiles. Levels are generated, so terrain stays code.

When ChatGPT drifts

  • It holds the style for 3-4 images, then slips. Start a fresh chat every batch, or every 3-4 images.
  • If it writes code or describes the image instead of making it, start a fresh chat. Arguing doesn't fix it.
  • Too busy? Reply: Too many parts. Half as many pieces, no small props, bigger simpler shapes. Same subject and view.
  • Too detailed or textured? Reply: Simpler, like the attached harbour. Chunky blocks, flat matte colours, no texture or lines. Same pose.
  • The header makes it restate the asset, view and style before each image. If that line is missing or wrong, the image will be too.
